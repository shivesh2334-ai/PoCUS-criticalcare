01 • Learning pathway

Five domains. One integrated scan.

Work left to right. Mark each domain complete; progress is stored on this device.

01
Recognise

Shock phenotypes

LV/RV pattern, pericardium, filling, distributive physiology and mixed shock. Integrate echo with exam and perfusion.

02
Quantify

VTI & haemodynamics

Obtain LVOT diameter and PW Doppler VTI. Trend stroke volume, cardiac output and response to intervention.

03
Test

Fluid responsiveness

Use passive leg raise, mini-fluid challenge or ventilator manoeuvres with a flow endpoint—not IVC alone.

04
Map

Lung ultrasound

Sliding, A-lines, B-lines, consolidation and pleural fluid. Apply BLUE thinking to acute respiratory failure.

05
Decongest

Systemic venous congestion

IVC plus hepatic, portal and intrarenal venous Doppler. Grade congestion in its clinical context.

02 • Bedside synthesis

Scan around a clinical question

PoCUS should refine probability and guide the next action. Repeat after an intervention; a single image is not a haemodynamic strategy.

STEP 1

Is there shock?

Perfusion, mentation, skin, urine output, lactate trend and arterial pressure.

STEP 2

What phenotype?

  • LV failure / hypovolaemia
  • RV pressure or volume stress
  • Tamponade / obstruction
  • Vasoplegia or mixed state
STEP 3

Will flow rise?

Perform a reversible preload test and measure change in VTI or another validated cardiac-output surrogate.

STEP 4

Will fluid harm?

Read lungs and venous Doppler. Balance responsiveness against tolerance and congestion.

SV ≈ LVOT area × VTI   •   CO = SV × HRTrend with the same view, sample position and technique. Small acquisition errors can create large apparent changes.

Practical competency ladder

1Observe: identify normal anatomy and standard views.
2Acquire: save interpretable loops in a structured sequence.
3Measure: reproduce VTI and Doppler waveforms reliably.
4Integrate: answer one clinical question using multiple organs.
5Audit: compare against expert review or formal imaging.
Suggested logbook: 30 supervised focused cardiac studies, 30 lung studies and 20 integrated shock assessments—then continue deliberate practice according to local credentialing standards.

05 • Safety & interpretation

Three guardrails

Do not treat a number.

VTI, IVC size and VExUS grade must be interpreted with the clinical phenotype, image quality and the trajectory after intervention.

Responsive ≠ needs fluid.

A patient may increase stroke volume yet have no indication for fluid—or may have poor fluid tolerance from pulmonary or venous congestion.

Escalate uncertainty.

Unexpected, discordant or technically limited findings should trigger expert review, formal echocardiography or alternative imaging.
